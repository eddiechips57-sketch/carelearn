Introduction • Proactive Protection

Creating a Safe Environment Without Restricting Freedom

As memory and cognitive processing change, familiar homes can suddenly present hidden hazards like hot gas hobs, slippery bathroom tiles, or confusing exit doors. This checklist helps you audit every room in 15 minutes.

Chapter 1 • Kitchen & Appliance Safety ↑ Back to Contents • Page 3
Chapter 1

Kitchen & Appliance Safety

The kitchen is the heart of the home, but open flames, hot kettles, and cleaning chemicals present major risks when memory fluctuates. Install a lockable gas isolation valve, automatic kettle shut-offs, and move cleaning products out of reach.

Chapter 2 • Bathroom & Fall Prevention ↑ Back to Contents • Page 4
Chapter 2

Bathroom & Fall Prevention

Fit vertical grab rails beside toilets and showers, use non-slip suction mats, and ensure lighting is bright enough to prevent shadows that look like holes in the floor.

Chapter 3 • Stairs, Hallways & Visual Optics ↑ Back to Contents • Page 5
Chapter 3

Stairs, Hallways & Visual Depth Perception

Dementia changes depth perception. Dark doormats look like deep holes, and shiny tiles look like puddles. Remove loose rugs and mark stair edges with high-contrast yellow tape.

Chapters 4 & 5 • Exits & Night Lighting ↑ Back to Contents
Chapters 4 & 5

Exits, Wandering & Night-Time Lighting

Keep plug-in motion nightlights along the path to the bathroom, and use door curtains or chime alerts to ensure safe night-time wandering response.
