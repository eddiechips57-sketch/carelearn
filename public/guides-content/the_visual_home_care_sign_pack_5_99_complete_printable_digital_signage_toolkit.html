Introduction • Restoring Independence

Helping the Brain Remember When Words Fade

As cognitive decline or visual impairment progresses, reading lengthy written instructions becomes confusing and frustrating. High-contrast visual signage bypasses damaged verbal memory pathways, allowing individuals to recognize familiar places like the toilet, bedroom, or kitchen instantly and maintain their dignity.

Chapter 1

The 3 Golden Rules of Dementia-Friendly Signage

1. Eye-Level Placement (1.2m) Never place signs too high up on doors. People with dementia often look slightly downward as they walk. Tape signs exactly 1.2 metres (4 feet) off the floor.
2. High Contrast (Yellow & Black) Black bold sans-serif text on a bright yellow or white background provides maximum contrast for ageing eyes with reduced visual acuity.
3. Combine Word + Picture Always pair clear uppercase lettering with a universal pictogram (like a toilet or bed icon) so comprehension is instantaneous.
